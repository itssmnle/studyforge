## 1. Laws of Indices

### What does this mean?
Rules used to simplify expressions involving powers (indices) with the same base.

![Diagram: Summary chart of Index Laws showing Multiplication Rule, Division Rule, Power of Power Rule, and Negative Power Rule](/maths-images/Y8/laws-of-indices-summary.webp)

---

### Method
* **Multiplication Rule:** Add indices ($x^a \times x^b = x^{a+b}$).
* **Division Rule:** Subtract indices ($x^a \div x^b = x^{a-b}$).
* **Power of a Power:** Multiply indices ($(x^a)^b = x^{a \times b}$).
* **Zero Index:** Any non-zero number to power $0$ equals $1$ ($x^0 = 1$).
* **Negative Index:** Take the reciprocal ($x^{-a} = \frac{1}{x^a}$).

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Simplify: 1) $y^5 \times y^3$, 2) $(2k^3)^4$

**Answer:**
1. Add indices: $y^{5+3} = \mathbf{y^8}$
2. Apply power to coefficient and index: $2^4 \times k^{3 \times 4} = 16k^{12}$

Answer: **1) $y^8$, 2) $16k^{12}$**

## 2. Percentage Change and Reverse Percentages

### What does this mean?
* **Percentage Change:** Expressing an increase or decrease as a percentage of the original amount.
* **Reverse Percentage:** Finding the original amount before a percentage change occurred.

---

### Method

#### Percentage Change:
$$\text{Percentage Change} = \frac{\text{Change}}{\text{Original}} \times 100$$

#### Reverse Percentages:
1. Represent original amount as $100\%$.
2. Calculate new percentage ($100\% + \text{Increase}\%$ or $100\% - \text{Decrease}\%$).
3. Divide final value by new percentage to find $1\%$, then multiply by $100$.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
A coat is reduced by $20\%$ in a sale to $\$64$. Find its original price.

**Answer:**
* New percentage $= 100\% - 20\% = 80\%$
* $80\% = \$64$
* $1\% = \$64 \div 80 = \$0.80$
* Original price ($100\%$) $= \$0.80 \times 100 = \$80$

Answer: **$\$80$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Never calculate $20\%$ of the sale price ($\$64$) and add it back! Reverse percentages must always start by equating the sale price to its new percentage of the original ($80\%$).
