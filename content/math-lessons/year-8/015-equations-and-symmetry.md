## 1. Plan and Elevation of 3D Shapes

### What does this mean?
Representing a 3D object using 2D drawings from three distinct viewpoints:
* **Plan View:** View looking directly from **above**.
* **Front Elevation:** View looking directly from the **front**.
* **Side Elevation:** View looking directly from the **side**.

![Diagram: 3D block model alongside its 2D Plan view (top), Front elevation, and Side elevation grid drawings](/maths-images/Y8/plan-front-side-elevations.png)

---

### Method
1. Look directly at the 3D shape from the specified direction without perspective distortion.
2. Count the number of visible grid blocks wide and high.
3. Draw the corresponding 2D grid outline.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Describe the Plan view of a cylinder resting on its circular base.

**Answer:**
Looking directly from above, you see a flat circle.

Answer: **A circle**

## 2. Transformations: Reflection

### What does this mean?
Flipping a shape across a **mirror line** ($x=a$, $y=b$, or $y=x$) so every point stays equidistant from the line.

![Diagram: Grid showing shape A reflected across mirror line x = 1 to form shape B](/maths-images/Y8/reflection-across-x-equals-1.png)

---

### Method
1. Identify the equation of the mirror line (e.g. $x = 2$ is a vertical line through $x=2$).
2. Count the perpendicular distance from each vertex of the shape to the mirror line.
3. Count the same distance on the opposite side of the mirror line to plot new vertices.
4. Join vertices to complete the reflected shape.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Reflect point $(1, 4)$ in the vertical mirror line $x = 3$.

**Answer:**
* Distance from $x = 1$ to mirror line $x = 3$ is $2$ units right.
* Move another $2$ units right from line $x = 3 \rightarrow x = 5$.
* $y$-coordinate remains $4$.

Answer: **$(5, 4)$**
