## 1. Expanding Double Brackets

### What does this mean?
Multiplying every term in the first bracket by every term in the second bracket to produce a quadratic expression.

![Diagram: FOIL method diagram expanding (x + 2)(x + 3) with curved arrows showing First, Outside, Inside, Last multiplications](/maths-images/Y8/foil-double-brackets.webp)

---

### Method
Use the **FOIL** method (**F**irst, **O**utside, **I**nside, **L**ast) or a grid:
1. Multiply **First** terms.
2. Multiply **Outside** terms.
3. Multiply **Inside** terms.
4. Multiply **Last** terms.
5. **Collect like terms** (combine middle terms).

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Expand and simplify $(x + 3)(x + 5)$.

**Answer:**
* **First:** $x \times x = x^2$
* **Outside:** $x \times 5 = 5x$
* **Inside:** $3 \times x = 3x$
* **Last:** $3 \times 5 = 15$
* Combine: $x^2 + 5x + 3x + 15 = x^2 + 8x + 15$

Answer: **$x^2 + 8x + 15$**

## 2. Factorising Expressions and Quadratics

### What does this mean?
Putting an expression back into brackets by dividing out common factors.
* **Single bracket factorising:** $3x + 6 = 3(x + 2)$
* **Quadratic factorising:** $x^2 + bx + c = (x + p)(x + q)$

---

### Method (Quadratic $x^2 + bx + c$):
1. Find two numbers $p$ and $q$ that **multiply to $c$** and **add to $b$**.
2. Write as $(x + p)(x + q)$.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Factorise $x^2 + 7x + 12$.

**Answer:**
* Factors of $12$: $1 \times 12$, $2 \times 6$, $3 \times 4$.
* Pair that adds to $7$: $3$ and $4$ ($3 + 4 = 7, 3 \times 4 = 12$).

Answer: **$(x + 3)(x + 4)$**

---

## 3. Solving Equations with Unknowns on Both Sides

### What does this mean?
Solving equations where the variable appears on both the left and right sides of the equals sign.

---

### Method
1. Eliminate the smaller variable term from both sides.
2. Solve the remaining two-step equation using inverse operations.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Solve $5x + 3 = 2x + 12$.

**Answer:**
1. Subtract $2x$ from both sides:
   $$3x + 3 = 12$$
2. Subtract $3$ from both sides:
   $$3x = 9$$
3. Divide both sides by $3$:
   $$x = 3$$

Answer: **$x = 3$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Always eliminate the smaller variable term first! If you have $5x$ and $-2x$, eliminate $-2x$ by adding $2x$ to both sides to keep coefficients positive.
