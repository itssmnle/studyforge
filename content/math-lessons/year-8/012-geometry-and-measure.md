## 1. Angles in Parallel Lines

### What does this mean?
When a line (transversal) crosses two parallel lines, specific equal or supplementary angle relationships are formed.

![Diagram: Parallel lines cut by a transversal showing alternate 'Z' angles equal, corresponding 'F' angles equal, and co-interior 'C' angles summing to 180](/maths-images/Y8/angles-in-parallel-lines.webp)

---

### Method
* **Alternate Angles:** Equal to each other (look for a **Z-shape**).
* **Corresponding Angles:** Equal to each other (look for an **F-shape**).
* **Co-interior Angles:** Add up to $180^\circ$ (look for a **C-shape**).

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Find angle $x$ given parallel lines with a co-interior angle of $110^\circ$.

**Answer:**
Co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$:
$$x = 180^\circ - 110^\circ = 70^\circ$$

Answer: **$x = 70^\circ$** *(Reason: Co-interior angles sum to $180^\circ$)*

## 2. Area of Trapezia and Circles

### What does this mean?
Calculating the area of trapezia and circles using standard geometric formulas.

![Diagram: Trapezium with parallel sides a and b and height h, next to a Circle with radius r and diameter d labeled](/maths-images/Y8/trapezium-and-circle-formulas.webp)

---

### Method

#### Area of Trapezium:
$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(a + b)h$$
*(Where $a$ and $b$ are parallel top/bottom sides, $h$ is perpendicular height).*

#### Area & Circumference of Circle:
$$\text{Area} = \pi r^2 \quad \Big| \quad \text{Circumference} = \pi d = 2\pi r$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Find the area of a circle with a radius of $5\text{ cm}$. Give your answer to $1\text{ decimal place}$.

**Answer:**
$$\text{Area} = \pi \times 5^2 = 25\pi \approx 78.5398\text{ cm}^2$$

Answer: **$78.5\text{ cm}^2$**
