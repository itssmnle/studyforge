## 1. Collecting Like Terms

### What does this mean?
**Simplifying** an algebraic expression by grouping terms that have the exact same variable parts.

![Diagram: Collecting like terms](/maths-images/Y7/10-collecting-like-terms.png)

---

### Method
1. Identify terms with matching variables (remember to include the sign in front of each term).
2. Add or subtract the numerical coefficients of like terms.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Simplify $4a + 3b + 2a - 5b$.

**Answer:**
* Group $a$ terms: $4a + 2a = 6a$
* Group $b$ terms: $+3b - 5b = -2b$

Answer: **$6a - 2b$**

---

## 2. Expanding Single Brackets

### What does this mean?
Multiplying the term outside the bracket by every term inside the bracket to remove the parentheses.

![Diagram: Area model showing single bracket expansion 3(2x + 4) split into rectangles 3*2x = 6x and 3*4 = 12](/maths-images/Y7/03-expanding-single-brackets.png)

---

### Method
1. Multiply term outside by the $1^{\text{st}}$ term inside.
2. Multiply term outside by the $2^{\text{nd}}$ term inside (paying attention to signs).

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Expand $3(2x - 5)$.

**Answer:**
* $3 \times 2x = 6x$
* $3 \times (-5) = -15$

Answer: **$6x - 15$**

---

## 3. Solving Linear Equations

### What does this mean?
Finding the specific value of an unknown variable that satisfies an algebraic equation.

---

### Method
1. Undo operations in reverse order using **inverse operations**.
2. Perform the same operation to **both sides** of the equation to keep it balanced.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Solve $4x + 3 = 19$.

**Answer:**
1. Subtract $3$ from both sides:
   $$4x = 16$$
2. Divide both sides by $4$:
   $$x = 4$$

Answer: **$x = 4$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Always substitute your answer back into the original equation to check! $4(4) + 3 = 16 + 3 = 19$. Perfect!
