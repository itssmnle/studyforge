## 1. Linear Sequences and Term-to-Term Rules

### What does this mean?
A **linear sequence** is a list of numbers that increases or decreases by the same amount each step. The **term-to-term rule** describes how to get from one number to the next.

![Diagram: Linear sequence 5, 9, 13, 17 with curved arrows between numbers showing '+4'](/maths-images/Y7/04-linear-sequences-term-to-term.webp)

---

### Method
1. Find the difference between consecutive terms by subtracting: $\text{Term}_2 - \text{Term}_1$.
2. Check if the difference is constant across all terms.
3. State the rule as "Add $d$" or "Subtract $d$".

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Find the term-to-term rule and the next two terms of the sequence: $7, 12, 17, 22, \dots$

**Answer:**
* Difference: $12 - 7 = +5$, $17 - 12 = +5$.
* Term-to-term rule: **Add $5$**.
* Next terms: $22 + 5 = 27$, $27 + 5 = 32$.

Answer: **Next terms are $27, 32$**

## 2. Position-to-Term Rule ($n^{\text{th}}$ Term)

### What does this mean?
The **$n^{\text{th}}$ term** rule is an algebraic formula that connects a term's position ($n$) to its value.

![Diagram: Position-to-term rule for a linear sequence](/maths-images/Y7/01-position-to-term-rule.webp)

---

### Method
1. Find the common difference $d$ between terms. This gives the coefficient of $n$ ($dn$).
2. Compare the $dn$ table to the sequence to find the zero term ($c = \text{Term}_1 - d$).
3. Write the formula as:

$$n^{\text{th}}\text{ term} = dn + c$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Find the $n^{\text{th}}$ term rule for the sequence $5, 8, 11, 14, \dots$

**Answer:**
1. Difference $d = +3 \rightarrow 3n$.
2. $3 \times 1 = 3$, but the first term is $5$. We need to add $2$ ($3 + 2 = 5$).
3. Formula: **$3n + 2$**.

Answer: **$3n + 2$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> To double-check your $n^{\text{th}}$ term rule, substitute $n = 1$: $3(1) + 2 = 5$ (matches 1st term!). Substitute $n = 2$: $3(2) + 2 = 8$ (matches 2nd term!).
