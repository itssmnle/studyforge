## 1. Factors, Multiples, and Primes

### What does this mean?
* **Factor:** A whole number that divides into another number with no remainder.
* **Multiple:** A number in another number's times table.
* **Prime Number:** A whole number greater than $1$ with exactly two factors: $1$ and itself ($2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, \dots$).

![Diagram: Factor rainbow diagram connecting pairs of factors for the number 24 (1x24, 2x12, 3x8, 4x6)](/maths-images/Y7/13-factors-multiples-primes.png)

---

### Method

#### Finding Factors (Factor Pairs):
1. Start with $1 \times \text{number}$.
2. Test $2, 3, 4, \dots$ in order to find pairs that multiply to give the target number.
3. Stop when factors repeat.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** List all factors of $24$.

**Answer:**
$1 \times 24 = 24$  
$2 \times 12 = 24$  
$3 \times 8 = 24$  
$4 \times 6 = 24$  

Answer: **$1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24$**

## 2. Square and Triangular Numbers

### What does this mean?
* **Square Numbers:** The result of multiplying an integer by itself ($1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, \dots$).
* **Triangular Numbers:** Numbers formed by adding consecutive integers ($1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, \dots$).

![Diagram: Visual dot patterns showing square numbers 1, 4, 9, 16 as square grids and triangular numbers 1, 3, 6, 10 arranged in triangles](/maths-images/Y7/14-square-and-triangular-numbers.png)

---

### Method
* To find the $n^{\text{th}}$ square number: calculate $n \times n = n^2$.
* To find the $n^{\text{th}}$ triangular number: add the first $n$ integers together, or use $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Find the $5^{\text{th}}$ square number and the $5^{\text{th}}$ triangular number.

**Answer:**
* $5^{\text{th}}$ Square number: $5^2 = 5 \times 5 = \mathbf{25}$
* $5^{\text{th}}$ Triangular number: $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 = \mathbf{15}$

## 3. Order of Operations (BIDMAS)

### What does this mean?
**BIDMAS** defines the correct order to carry out operations in mathematical calculations:

1. **B**rackets $( )$
2. **I**ndices $x^2$
3. **D**ivision $\div$ & **M**ultiplication $\times$
4. **A**ddition $+$ & **S**ubtraction $-$

---

### Method
1. Evaluate expressions inside **Brackets** first.
2. Calculate **Indices** (powers/roots).
3. Perform **Division** and **Multiplication** from left to right.
4. Perform **Addition** and **Subtraction** from left to right.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:** Calculate $5 + 3 \times (4^2 - 6)$.

**Answer:**
1. Inside brackets ($4^2 - 6$): Calculate index first $\rightarrow 16 - 6 = 10$.
2. Expression becomes: $5 + 3 \times 10$.
3. Perform multiplication: $3 \times 10 = 30$.
4. Perform addition: $5 + 30 = 35$.

Answer: **$35$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Multiplication does NOT automatically take priority over Division, nor Addition over Subtraction! Work Division/Multiplication from left to right as they appear.
