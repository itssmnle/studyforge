## 1. Factorising Quadratics ($ax^2 + bx + c$)

### What does this mean?
Putting a quadratic expression with $a > 1$ into two brackets $(px + q)(rx + s)$.

![Diagram: Grid method diagram showing 2x^2 + 7x + 3 split into 2x^2, 6x, 1x, 3 to factorise as (2x + 1)(x + 3)](/maths-images/Y9/grid-method-factorisation.webp)

---

### Method
1. Find two numbers that **multiply to $a \times c$** and **add to $b$**.
2. Split the middle $bx$ term into these two terms.
3. Factorise by grouping in pairs.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Factorise $2x^2 + 7x + 3$.

**Answer:**
* $a \times c = 2 \times 3 = 6$, $b = 7$.
* Two numbers that multiply to $6$ and add to $7$: $6$ and $1$.
* Split middle term: $2x^2 + 6x + 1x + 3$
* Factorise pairs: $2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3)$
* Take out common bracket $(x + 3)$:

Answer: **$(2x + 1)(x + 3)$**

## 2. Solving Quadratic Equations by Factorisation

### What does this mean?
Finding the real values of $x$ (roots) where a quadratic equation equals zero ($ax^2 + bx + c = 0$).

---

### Method
1. Rearrange the equation so one side equals **zero** ($0$).
2. Factorise the quadratic into two brackets $(x + p)(x + q) = 0$.
3. Set each bracket to zero independently to find the two solutions ($x = -p$ or $x = -q$).

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$.

**Answer:**
1. Factorise: Numbers that multiply to $+6$ and add to $-5$ are $-2$ and $-3$.
2. $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$
3. Either $x - 2 = 0 \implies x = 2$ or $x - 3 = 0 \implies x = 3$.

Answer: **$x = 2$ or $x = 3$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Quadratic equations usually have **two** solutions! Never divide both sides by $x$ to simplify (e.g., $x^2 = 5x \implies x = 5$), as you will lose the solution $x = 0$. Always set to zero and factorise instead.

## 3. Rearranging Complex Formulae

### What does this mean?
Changing the subject of a formula when the target variable appears multiple times, inside brackets, or under square roots.

---

### Method
1. Remove fractions by multiplying by the denominator.
2. Expand any brackets containing the target variable.
3. Collect all terms containing the target variable on **one side** of the equals sign.
4. Move all other terms to the **opposite side**.
5. **Factorise** out the target variable and divide.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Make $x$ the subject of $y = \frac{x + 2}{x - 3}$.

**Answer:**
1. Multiply by $(x - 3)$: $y(x - 3) = x + 2$
2. Expand bracket: $xy - 3y = x + 2$
3. Collect $x$ terms on left: $xy - x = 3y + 2$
4. Factorise $x$: $x(y - 1) = 3y + 2$
5. Divide by $(y - 1)$:

Answer: **$x = \frac{3y + 2}{y - 1}$**
