## 1. Repeated Percentage Change and Compound Interest

### What does this mean?
**Compound interest** means calculating percentage growth where interest earned in each period is added to the principal total before calculating the next period's interest.

$$\text{Final Amount} = \text{Initial Amount} \times (\text{Multiplier})^n$$

* $n$ = number of years / time periods

![Diagram: Exponential growth curve showing compound interest vs linear growth curve showing simple interest over time](/maths-images/Y9/compound-vs-simple-interest.webp)

---

### Method
1. Convert percentage rate into a decimal multiplier (e.g., $+5\% \rightarrow 1.05$).
2. Raise multiplier to the power of $n$ (where $n$ is the number of time periods).
3. Multiply initial amount by $(\text{Multiplier})^n$.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Invest $\$2000$ at $4\%$ compound interest per year for $3$ years. Calculate the final balance.

**Answer:**
* Multiplier $= 100\% + 4\% = 104\% = 1.04$
* $n = 3\text{ years}$
* $\text{Final Balance} = 2000 \times (1.04)^3 = 2000 \times 1.124864 = \$2249.73$

Answer: **$\$2249.73$**

## 2. Simple Interest and Exchange Rates

### What does this mean?
* **Simple Interest:** Interest calculated solely on the original principal amount every year ($\text{Interest} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$).
* **Exchange Rates:** Converting money between currencies using direct proportion multiplication or division.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Convert $\pounds 400$ into Euros (€) using the exchange rate $\pounds 1 = \text{€}1.15$.

**Answer:**
$$\pounds 400 \times 1.15 = \text{€}460$$

Answer: **$\text{€}460$**
