## 1. Finding the Equation of a Line ($y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$)

### What does this mean?
Determining the exact algebraic equation of a straight line given its gradient $m$ and any point $(x_1, y_1)$ through which it passes.

![Diagram: Line graph passing through point (2, 5) with slope rise over run equal to 3](/maths-images/Y9/line-gradient-rise-over-run.png)

---

### Method
1. Calculate the gradient $m$:

$$m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$

2. Substitute $m$ and point $(x_1, y_1)$ into:

$$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$$

3. Expand and rearrange into $y = mx + c$ or $ax + by + c = 0$.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Find the equation of the line passing through $(2, 5)$ with a gradient of $3$.

**Answer:**
1. $m = 3$, $(x_1, y_1) = (2, 5)$
2. $y - 5 = 3(x - 2)$
3. $y - 5 = 3x - 6 \implies y = 3x - 1$

Answer: **$y = 3x - 1$**

## 2. Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

### What does this mean?
* **Parallel lines** have the **same gradient** ($m_1 = m_2$).
* **Perpendicular lines** meet at right angles ($90^\circ$). Their gradients are **negative reciprocals**:

$$m_1 \times m_2 = -1 \quad \implies \quad m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$$

![Diagram: Cartesian plane showing parallel lines with equal slopes m = 2, and a perpendicular line with slope m = -1/2](/maths-images/Y9/parallel-and-perpendicular-slopes.png)

---

### Method
1. Identify the gradient $m_1$ of the given line.
2. For a perpendicular line, turn $m_1$ into a fraction, flip it upside down, and change its sign to get $m_2$.
3. Use $y - y_1 = m_2(x - x_1)$ to find the new line equation.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Find the gradient of a line perpendicular to $y = 4x - 5$.

**Answer:**
* Given gradient $m_1 = 4 = \frac{4}{1}$
* Negative reciprocal $m_2 = -\frac{1}{4}$

Answer: **$-\frac{1}{4}$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> To check if two lines are perpendicular, multiply their gradients together! If $m_1 \times m_2 = -1$, they are guaranteed to be perpendicular.
