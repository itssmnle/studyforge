## A Balanced Diet

![Illustration of a balanced meal](/note-images/biology/balanced-diet.png "Placeholder illustration: a balanced diet")

A healthy human diet must contain seven essential components. Each one plays a specific role in keeping the body functioning properly.

**Carbohydrates** provide energy. They are found in foods like bread, rice, and pasta.

**Lipids** provide energy and insulation. They are found in butter, oils, and nuts.

**Proteins** are needed for growth and repair. They are found in meat, fish, eggs, and beans.

**Vitamins** are needed in small amounts for various body functions. For example, vitamin C helps heal wounds and vitamin D helps absorb calcium for strong bones.

**Minerals** are also needed in small amounts. For example, calcium is needed for strong bones and teeth, and iron is needed to make haemoglobin in red blood cells.

**Dietary fibre** helps food move through the digestive system. It prevents constipation. It is found in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains.

**Water** is essential for all life processes. It makes up a large proportion of the body and is needed for chemical reactions to take place.

### Energy Requirements

Different people need different amounts of energy each day. The amount of energy a person needs depends on several factors. These include their age, gender, activity level, and even the external temperature.

Energy requirements are measured in **kilojoules (kJ)** or **kilocalories (kcal)** . To calculate daily energy needs, you can use charts and tables that estimate requirements based on these factors. If a person consumes more energy than they use, they will gain weight. If they consume less, they will lose weight.

## Imbalances in Diet

![Illustration of food groups in a balanced diet](/note-images/biology/balanced-diet.png "Placeholder illustration: imbalances in diet")

When a person's diet is not balanced, it can lead to health problems.

**Obesity** occurs when a person consumes more energy than they use, over a long period. The excess energy is stored as fat. Obesity increases the risk of serious health conditions, including type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and joint problems.

**Starvation** occurs when a person does not consume enough food to meet their energy needs. The body begins to break down its own tissues for energy. This leads to extreme weight loss, weakness, and damage to vital organs.

**Deficiency diseases** occur when a person does not get enough of a specific vitamin or mineral. For example, a lack of vitamin D and calcium can lead to rickets, which causes soft and weakened bones. A lack of iron can lead to anaemia, which causes tiredness and pale skin.

## The Digestive System

![Illustration of digestion and enzymes](/note-images/biology/digestion-and-enzymes.png "Placeholder illustration: the digestive system")

The digestive system is a long tube that processes food and absorbs nutrients. Food is broken down physically and chemically as it travels through the system.

The journey begins in the **mouth**, where teeth chew food into smaller pieces. **Saliva** is added, which contains an enzyme that begins to break down starch.

Food then travels down the **oesophagus** to the **stomach**. In the stomach, food is churned and mixed with strong acid and enzymes. The acid kills harmful bacteria and helps enzymes work.

Next, food moves into the **small intestine**. Here, most digestion and absorption happen. Enzymes from the pancreas and the intestinal wall break down food into small molecules. These molecules are absorbed through the intestinal wall into the bloodstream.

The remaining material moves into the **large intestine**, where water is absorbed. The leftover solid waste is stored in the **rectum** before being removed from the body through the **anus**.

### Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts. This means they speed up chemical reactions in the body without being used up themselves. Digestive enzymes break down large, insoluble food molecules into smaller, soluble molecules that can be absorbed.

Different enzymes break down different food groups. For example, **protease** breaks down proteins into amino acids, **lipase** breaks down lipids into fatty acids and glycerol, and **amylase** breaks down starch into sugars.

### Gut Bacteria

The digestive system contains trillions of bacteria. These are often called gut bacteria or the gut microbiome. They are essential for health. They help to digest some foods that our own enzymes cannot break down. They also produce certain vitamins, such as vitamin K. Furthermore, they help protect against harmful bacteria by competing for space and nutrients.

## Health and Lifestyle

![Illustration of healthy lifestyle choices](/note-images/biology/health-and-lifestyle.png "Placeholder illustration: health and lifestyle")

A person's lifestyle can have a significant impact on their health.

Recreational drugs are substances taken for their effects on the body and mind. Some, like alcohol and nicotine, are legal but still harmful. Others, like heroin and cocaine, are illegal.

**Alcohol** is a depressant, meaning it slows down the brain and nervous system. It can damage the liver and heart over time. It also impairs judgement and coordination.

**Nicotine** is found in tobacco products. It is highly addictive. Smoking damages the lungs and increases the risk of lung cancer, heart disease, and other respiratory problems.

**Substance misuse** refers to the harmful or hazardous use of any drug, including alcohol and prescription medicines. It can lead to addiction, mental health problems, organ damage, and social consequences.

The effects of drug misuse can be physical, such as organ failure and disease. They can also be behavioural, such as poor decision-making, risk-taking, and changes in mood and personality.
