## The Skeletal System

![Illustration of the human skeleton](/note-images/biology/the-skeleton.png "Placeholder illustration: the skeletal system")

The human skeleton is a strong but lightweight framework made of bones. It has several vital functions.

The skeleton provides **support** for the body, giving it shape and holding everything in place. It offers **protection** for vital organs. For example, the skull protects the brain and the ribcage protects the heart and lungs. The skeleton allows **movement** because muscles attach to bones and pull on them to create motion. Additionally, some bones contain **bone marrow**, which produces new blood cells.

## Joints

![Illustration of a working joint](/note-images/biology/joints.png "Placeholder illustration: joints")

A joint is where two or more bones meet. Different joints allow different types of movement.

**Fixed joints** do not move. The bones in the skull are joined this way.

**Slightly movable joints** allow a small amount of movement. The joints between the vertebrae in the spine are examples.

**Synovial joints** are freely movable. They are the most common type in the body. Synovial joints contain a slippery liquid called synovial fluid, which reduces friction and allows smooth movement. The knee, elbow, and shoulder are all synovial joints.

## The Muscular System

![Illustration of muscles moving an arm](/note-images/biology/muscles-and-movement.png "Placeholder illustration: the muscular system")

Muscles are tissues that can contract, or get shorter. When a muscle contracts, it pulls on a bone, causing movement.

Muscles are attached to bones by strong cords called **tendons**.

Muscles can only pull; they cannot push. Therefore, they must work in pairs to create movement in two directions. These are called **antagonistic pairs**.

### Antagonistic Muscle Pairs

The **biceps** and **triceps** in the upper arm are a classic antagonistic pair.

To bend the elbow, the biceps contracts and shortens, pulling the forearm upwards. At the same time, the triceps relaxes and lengthens.

To straighten the elbow, the triceps contracts and shortens, pulling the forearm downwards. At the same time, the biceps relaxes and lengthens.

## Biomechanics

![Illustration of muscles and bones creating movement](/note-images/biology/muscles-and-movement.png "Placeholder illustration: biomechanics")

Biomechanics is the study of how the skeleton and muscles interact to produce movement.

Muscles exert a force when they contract. This force can be measured. The size of the force a muscle produces depends on several factors. These include the size of the muscle and the number of muscle fibres that are stimulated to contract.

A **lever system** is involved in body movements. The bone acts as a lever, the joint acts as the pivot or fulcrum, and the muscle provides the effort to move the load (the weight of the body part or an object being lifted).
