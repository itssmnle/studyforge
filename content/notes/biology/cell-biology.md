## Cell Structure

![Illustration of a cell under examination](/note-images/biology/cell-structure.png "Placeholder illustration: cell structure")

All living things are made of cells. Cells are the tiny building blocks of life.

A **light microscope** lets you see cells. You need to know how to use it, draw what you see, and record your observations carefully.

There are two main types of cells:

- **Eukaryotic cells** are complex cells. Plants and animals are made of these.
- **Prokaryotic cells** are simpler and smaller. Bacteria are prokaryotes. They are **unicellular**, meaning they are made of just one cell.

## Organelles and Their Functions

![Illustration of a cell organelle](/note-images/biology/cell-structure.png "Placeholder illustration: cell organelles")

Organelles are parts inside a cell, each with a specific job. Think of them like tiny organs.

**Found in both plant and animal cells**

**Nucleus** controls the cell. It contains DNA, which is the genetic information.

**Cytoplasm** is a jelly-like substance. Most chemical reactions happen here.

**Cell membrane** surrounds the cell. It controls what enters and leaves.

**Mitochondria** are the powerhouses. They release energy from food through respiration.

**Ribosomes** make proteins. Proteins are needed for growth and repair.

**Found only in plant cells**

**Cell wall** is a rigid outer layer. It is made of cellulose and supports the cell.

**Permanent vacuole** is a large sac filled with cell sap. It stores nutrients and helps keep the cell firm.

**Chloroplasts** absorb sunlight to make food through photosynthesis. They contain a green pigment called chlorophyll.

## Diffusion

![Illustration of particles moving across a membrane](/note-images/biology/transport-and-specialisation.png "Placeholder illustration: diffusion")

Diffusion is the movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration. It is a passive process, meaning no energy is needed.

This is how substances like oxygen, carbon dioxide, and nutrients move into and out of cells.

## Specialised Cells

![Illustration of cells adapted for their jobs](/note-images/biology/transport-and-specialisation.png "Placeholder illustration: specialised cells")

Some cells have special features that help them do a particular job. This is called adaptation.

**Sperm cell** has a tail for swimming and many mitochondria to provide energy. It is adapted for reproduction.

**Red blood cell** has a biconcave shape to increase surface area. It contains haemoglobin to carry oxygen. It has no nucleus to leave more room for haemoglobin.

**Root hair cell** has a long projection to increase surface area for absorbing water and minerals from the soil.

## Levels of Organisation

![Illustration of cells, tissues, organs, and organ systems](/note-images/biology/levels-of-organisation.png "Placeholder illustration: levels of organisation")

Multicellular organisms are built in a hierarchy. This means they are organised from simple to complex, in five levels:

1. **Cells** are the basic unit. For example, a muscle cell.

2. **Tissues** are groups of similar cells working together. For example, muscle tissue.

3. **Organs** are made of different tissues working together. For example, the heart.

4. **Organ systems** are groups of organs working together. For example, the circulatory system.

5. **Organism** is a complete living thing made of many organ systems. For example, a human.
