## The Gas Exchange System

![Illustration of gas exchange in the lungs](/note-images/biology/gas-exchange.png "Placeholder illustration: gas exchange")

The gas exchange system, also known as the breathing system or respiratory system, is responsible for taking in oxygen and removing carbon dioxide.

Air enters through the nose or mouth and travels down the **trachea**, or windpipe. The trachea is supported by rings of cartilage, which keep it open. It splits into two tubes called **bronchi**. Each bronchus leads to one lung. Inside the lungs, the bronchi divide into smaller and smaller tubes called **bronchioles**. At the end of the bronchioles are tiny air sacs called **alveoli**.

The **alveoli** are the site of gas exchange. They have very thin walls, only one cell thick, which allows gases to pass through easily. They are surrounded by a network of tiny blood vessels called capillaries. Oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveoli to be breathed out. The alveoli provide a very large surface area for efficient gas exchange.

## The Mechanism of Breathing

![Illustration of the lungs during breathing](/note-images/biology/gas-exchange.png "Placeholder illustration: breathing")

Breathing, also called ventilation, is the process of moving air into and out of the lungs. It is driven by changes in pressure caused by the movement of the diaphragm and ribs.

**Inhalation, or breathing in, happens when the diaphragm contracts and flattens.** The ribs move upwards and outwards. This increases the volume of the chest cavity. As the volume increases, the pressure inside the lungs decreases. Air flows in from the outside, where the pressure is higher, to equalise the pressure.

**Exhalation, or breathing out, is usually a passive process.** The diaphragm relaxes and curves upwards. The ribs move downwards and inwards. This decreases the volume of the chest cavity. As the volume decreases, the pressure inside the lungs increases. Air is forced out to the outside, where the pressure is lower.

## Lung Volume

![Illustration of the lungs](/note-images/biology/gas-exchange.png "Placeholder illustration: lung volume")

Lung volume is the amount of air the lungs can hold. It can be measured using a simple device called a spirometer or by using a peak flow meter.

Different people have different lung volumes. Factors that affect lung volume include age, gender, height, and fitness level.

**Exercise** increases the rate and depth of breathing. Over time, regular exercise strengthens the breathing muscles and can increase vital capacity, which is the maximum amount of air a person can breathe out after a deep breath in.

**Asthma** is a condition that affects the airways. The bronchioles become inflamed and narrow, making it difficult to breathe. The airways may also produce excess mucus. This reduces the flow of air and can cause wheezing, coughing, and shortness of breath.

**Smoking** damages the gas exchange system in several ways. It irritates and damages the lining of the trachea and bronchi. It destroys the tiny hairs called cilia that sweep mucus out of the airways. This leads to a build-up of mucus, causing a persistent cough. Smoking also damages the alveoli, reducing their elasticity and surface area, which makes gas exchange less efficient. Over time, smoking can lead to serious conditions such as chronic bronchitis, emphysema, and lung cancer.

## Respiration

![Illustration of aerobic respiration in a cell](/note-images/biology/aerobic-respiration.png "Placeholder illustration: respiration")

Respiration is the chemical reaction that releases energy from glucose. It happens in every living cell. Energy is needed for all life processes, including muscle contraction, growth, repair, and keeping warm.

### Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic respiration requires oxygen. It releases a large amount of energy. It happens continuously in cells when there is enough oxygen available.

The word equation for aerobic respiration is:

$$\text{Glucose} + \text{Oxygen} \rightarrow \text{Carbon dioxide} + \text{Water}$$

The balanced chemical symbol equation is:

$$\ce{C6H12O6 + 6O2 -> 6CO2 + 6H2O}$$

This reaction occurs in the mitochondria of cells.

### Anaerobic Respiration

Anaerobic respiration releases energy without oxygen. It releases much less energy than aerobic respiration. The body only uses anaerobic respiration when aerobic respiration cannot supply energy quickly enough.

In humans, anaerobic respiration happens during intense exercise when the muscles are working very hard. It produces a substance called **lactic acid**. The build-up of lactic acid causes a burning sensation in the muscles and leads to muscle fatigue.

The word equation for anaerobic respiration in humans is:

$$\text{Glucose} \rightarrow \text{Lactic acid}$$

After exercise, the lactic acid must be broken down. This requires oxygen. The amount of oxygen needed is called the **oxygen debt**. This is why a person continues to breathe deeply and rapidly after stopping exercise. It is often called repaying the oxygen debt.

In micro-organisms such as yeast, anaerobic respiration is called **fermentation**. It produces ethanol and carbon dioxide. This process is used in brewing and baking.

The word equation for fermentation in yeast is:

$$\text{Glucose} \rightarrow \text{Ethanol} + \text{Carbon dioxide}$$
