## Energy Stores and Transfers

![Illustration of energy stores and transfers](/note-images/physics/energy-stores-and-transfers.png "Placeholder illustration: energy stores and transfers")

Energy is the ability to do work. It is measured in joules (J).

There are several different **energy stores**. Kinetic energy is the energy of moving objects. Gravitational potential energy is the energy stored in objects raised above the ground. Chemical energy is stored in fuels, food, and batteries. Elastic potential energy is stored in stretched or compressed springs. Thermal energy is the energy stored in hot objects. Electrostatic, magnetic, and nuclear energy are other stores.

Energy can be transferred between stores through different **pathways**. Energy can be transferred mechanically, such as when a force moves an object. It can be transferred electrically, such as when a current flows through a wire. It can be transferred by heating, such as when a hot object warms a cold object. It can also be transferred by radiation, such as light or sound waves.

## Conservation of Energy

![Illustration of energy moving between stores](/note-images/physics/energy-stores-and-transfers.png "Placeholder illustration: conservation of energy")

The law of **conservation of energy** states that energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one store to another. The total amount of energy in a closed system remains constant. This means that the total energy before a transfer is equal to the total energy after the transfer.

When energy is transferred, some energy is often dissipated, or wasted, as thermal energy. For example, in a car engine, not all of the chemical energy in fuel is transferred to kinetic energy. Some is wasted as heat in the engine and exhaust.

## Work and Power

![Illustration of work and power](/note-images/physics/work-and-power.png "Placeholder illustration: work and power")

**Work** is done when a force moves an object. It is a measure of the energy transferred. Work is measured in joules (J) and is calculated using the equation:

$$W = Fd$$

The distance is measured in the direction of the force.

**Power** is the rate at which energy is transferred. It is measured in watts (W), where one watt is one joule per second. Power is calculated using the equation:

$$P = \frac{E}{t}$$

A higher power rating means energy is transferred more quickly.

## Domestic Fuel Use and Costs

![Illustration of heating and energy use](/note-images/physics/heating-and-energy-use.png "Placeholder illustration: domestic fuel use and costs")

Electricity usage in homes is measured in **kilowatt-hours (kWh)** . This is the amount of energy used by a 1 kW appliance running for 1 hour. The equation is:

$$E_{\mathrm{kWh}} = P_{\mathrm{kW}}t_{\mathrm{hours}}$$

The cost of using an appliance can be calculated by multiplying the energy used by the cost per kWh. Different appliances have different power ratings. A higher power rating uses more energy in the same amount of time. Comparing power ratings and fuel costs helps consumers choose the most efficient appliances.

## Heating and Thermal Equilibrium

![Illustration of heating and energy use](/note-images/physics/heating-and-energy-use.png "Placeholder illustration: heating and thermal equilibrium")

Heat energy always flows from a hotter object to a colder object. The energy flows until both objects reach the same temperature. This is called **thermal equilibrium**.

**Conduction** is the transfer of heat energy through a solid. Metals are good conductors of heat. Non-metals, such as wood and plastic, are poor conductors and are good thermal insulators.

**Convection** is the transfer of heat energy through fluids, such as liquids and gases. The warmer fluid expands, becomes less dense, and rises. The cooler fluid sinks and takes its place.

**Radiation** is the transfer of heat energy by electromagnetic waves. It does not require a medium and can travel through a vacuum.

**Thermal insulators** are used to reduce the rate of heat transfer. Examples include loft insulation, double glazing, and vacuum flasks.
