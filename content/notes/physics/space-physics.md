## Earth, Sun, and Moon

![Illustration of Earth, Sun, and Moon](/note-images/physics/earth-sun-and-moon.png "Placeholder illustration: Earth, Sun, and Moon")

The Earth rotates on its own axis. One complete rotation takes approximately 24 hours. This rotation causes **day and night**. The side of the Earth facing the Sun experiences day. The side facing away from the Sun experiences night.

The Earth orbits the Sun. One complete orbit takes approximately 365.25 days. This is one year.

The Moon orbits the Earth. One complete orbit takes approximately 27.3 days. The Moon does not produce its own light. It reflects light from the Sun. The different phases of the Moon are caused by the changing position of the Moon relative to the Earth and the Sun.

## Seasons

![Illustration of Earth's seasons](/note-images/physics/seasons.png "Placeholder illustration: seasons")

The **seasons** are caused by the tilt of the Earth's axis. The axis is tilted at an angle of approximately 23.5 degrees from the vertical. This tilt means that different parts of the Earth receive different amounts of sunlight at different times of year.

When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, it receives more direct sunlight and experiences summer. At the same time, the Southern Hemisphere experiences winter. When the Northern Hemisphere is tilted away from the Sun, it experiences winter and the Southern Hemisphere experiences summer. The equinoxes occur when the tilt is side-on to the Sun, giving equal day and night.

## Solar System and Beyond

![Illustration of the Solar System](/note-images/physics/solar-system-and-beyond.png "Placeholder illustration: Solar System and beyond")

The Solar System consists of the Sun and everything that orbits it. There are eight **planets**. The four inner planets, Mercury, Venus, Earth, and Mars, are rocky planets. The four outer planets, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune, are gas giants.

The planets orbit the Sun in roughly circular paths called orbits. They travel in the same direction around the Sun. The further away a planet is from the Sun, the longer its orbit takes.

The Solar System also contains moons, asteroids, comets, and dwarf planets. Pluto is now classified as a dwarf planet.

The Sun is a **star**. It is one of billions of stars in the **Milky Way** galaxy. A galaxy is a huge collection of stars, gas, and dust. There are billions of galaxies in the observable universe.

Distances in space are enormous. They are measured in **light years**. One light year is the distance that light travels in one year. It is approximately 9.46 trillion kilometres.
