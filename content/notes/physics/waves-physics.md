## Wave Properties

![Illustration of wave properties](/note-images/physics/wave-properties.png "Placeholder illustration: wave properties")

Waves transfer energy from one place to another without transferring matter.

**Amplitude** is the maximum displacement of a point on the wave from its rest position. It is a measure of the wave's energy. A larger amplitude means more energy.

**Wavelength** is the distance between two corresponding points on adjacent waves, such as from crest to crest or trough to trough.

**Frequency** is the number of waves passing a point per second. It is measured in hertz (Hz). A higher frequency means more waves pass per second.

The speed of a wave can be calculated using the equation:

$$v = f\lambda$$

## Sound Waves

![Illustration of sound waves](/note-images/physics/sound.png "Placeholder illustration: sound waves")

Sound is a longitudinal wave. The particles in the medium vibrate back and forth in the same direction as the wave travels. This creates compressions and rarefactions. Sound requires a physical medium to travel through. It cannot travel through a vacuum because there are no particles to vibrate.

The **pitch** of a sound depends on its frequency. A higher frequency produces a higher pitch. The **volume** of a sound depends on its amplitude. A larger amplitude produces a louder sound.

The human auditory range is approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Sounds above this range are called ultrasound. Sounds below this range are called infrasound.

## Light Waves

![Illustration of light waves](/note-images/physics/light-and-colour.png "Placeholder illustration: light waves")

Light is a transverse wave. The vibrations are at right angles to the direction of travel. Light can travel through a vacuum, unlike sound. The speed of light in a vacuum is approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

**Reflection** occurs when light bounces off a surface. The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.

**Refraction** occurs when light changes direction as it passes from one medium to another of different density. When light enters a denser medium, it bends towards the normal. When it enters a less dense medium, it bends away from the normal. This bending causes objects to appear bent when viewed through water.

**Ray diagrams** are used to show the path of light. **Convex lenses** converge light rays to a focal point. They are used in magnifying glasses and to correct long-sightedness. The human eye uses a convex lens to focus light onto the retina.

A **pinhole camera** is a simple device that forms an inverted image. The size of the image depends on the distance between the pinhole and the screen.

## Colour

![Illustration of light and colour](/note-images/physics/light-and-colour.png "Placeholder illustration: colour")

**White light** can be dispersed into a spectrum of colours using a prism. The colours of the spectrum are red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet. Each colour has a different wavelength.

The **primary colours** of light are red, green, and blue. Mixing equal amounts of these three colours produces white light. Mixing two primary colours produces a secondary colour. For example, red and green light together produce yellow.

The colour of an object depends on the light it reflects. A red object reflects red light and absorbs other colours. A white object reflects all colours. A black object absorbs all colours.
