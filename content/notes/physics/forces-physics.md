## Describing Motion

![Illustration of describing motion](/note-images/physics/describing-motion.png "Placeholder illustration: describing motion")

**Speed** is a measure of how fast an object is moving. It can be calculated using the equation:

$$v = \frac{d}{t}$$

Speed is usually measured in metres per second (m/s) or kilometres per hour (km/h).

A **distance-time graph** shows how far an object has travelled over time. The gradient, or slope, of the line represents the speed. A steeper gradient means a higher speed. A horizontal line means the object is stationary. A curved line means the object is accelerating or decelerating.

**Relative motion** describes how the speed of one object appears compared to another moving object. When two objects move in the same direction, their relative speed is the difference between their speeds. When they move in opposite directions, their relative speed is the sum of their speeds.

## Gravity, Mass, and Weight

![Illustration of forces acting on an object](/note-images/physics/forces.png "Placeholder illustration: gravity, mass, and weight")

**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object. It is measured in kilograms (kg). Mass does not change depending on location.

**Weight** is the force of gravity acting on an object. It is measured in newtons (N). Weight depends on the gravitational field strength. On Earth, the gravitational field strength is approximately 10 N/kg.

Weight can be calculated using the equation:

$$W = mg$$

An object with a mass of 5 kg has a weight of 50 N on Earth.

## Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

![Illustration of balanced and unbalanced forces](/note-images/physics/forces.png "Placeholder illustration: balanced and unbalanced forces")

Forces are pushes or pulls. They can be represented by arrows. The arrow shows the direction of the force and its size.

When forces on an object are **balanced**, the object is in equilibrium. It will either remain stationary or continue moving at a constant speed. The resultant force is zero.

When forces on an object are **unbalanced**, the object will accelerate, decelerate, or change direction. The resultant force is not zero. A larger resultant force produces a greater acceleration.

## Contact Forces

![Illustration of contact forces](/note-images/physics/forces.png "Placeholder illustration: contact forces")

**Friction** is a force that opposes motion between two surfaces in contact. It can be useful, such as when walking or braking. It can also be wasteful, such as when it causes wear and heat.

**Air resistance** and **water resistance** are types of friction that act on objects moving through fluids. They increase with speed.

**Tension** is the pulling force exerted by a string, rope, or cable when it is stretched.

**Springs** obey Hooke's Law. This states that the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the force applied, up to the limit of proportionality. The equation is:

$$F = kx$$

## Moments

![Illustration of moments and pressure](/note-images/physics/moments-and-pressure.png "Placeholder illustration: moments")

A **moment** is the turning effect of a force. It is calculated using the equation:

$$M = Fd_{\perp}$$

The distance is measured from the pivot, or turning point, to the line of action of the force. Moments are measured in newton-metres (Nm). A larger force or a larger distance from the pivot creates a larger moment.

For an object to be balanced, the total clockwise moment must equal the total anticlockwise moment.

## Pressure in Fluids

![Illustration of moments and pressure](/note-images/physics/moments-and-pressure.png "Placeholder illustration: pressure in fluids")

**Pressure** is the force exerted per unit area. It is calculated using the equation:

$$p = \frac{F}{A}$$

Pressure is measured in pascals (Pa) or newtons per square metre (N/m²). A smaller area produces a larger pressure for the same force. This is why sharp knives cut more easily.

**Atmospheric pressure** decreases with altitude. As you go higher, there are fewer air particles above you, so the pressure is lower.

**Liquid pressure** increases with depth. The deeper you go, the more liquid particles are above you, pressing down. Liquid pressure also increases with density.

**Upthrust** is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an object. If the upthrust is greater than the weight of the object, the object floats. If the weight is greater than the upthrust, the object sinks.
