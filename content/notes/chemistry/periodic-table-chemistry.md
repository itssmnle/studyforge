## Structure of the Periodic Table

![Illustration of the periodic table](/note-images/chemistry/periodic-table-structure.png "Placeholder illustration: structure of the periodic table")

The **Periodic Table** is a way of organising all the known chemical elements. Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number. The atomic number is the number of protons in an atom.

The table is divided into vertical columns called **groups** and horizontal rows called **periods**. Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties because they have the same number of electrons in their outer shell. Elements in the same period have the same number of electron shells.

---

## Metals and Non-metals

![Illustration comparing metals and non-metals](/note-images/chemistry/metals-and-non-metals.png "Placeholder illustration: metals and non-metals")

The Periodic Table is divided into **metals** and **non-metals**. Metals are found on the left and centre of the table. Non-metals are found on the right.

Metals have characteristic physical properties. They are shiny, good conductors of heat and electricity, malleable, and ductile. Non-metals are usually dull, poor conductors, and brittle when solid.

Metals and non-metals also differ in their chemical properties. Metal oxides are basic and will neutralise acids. Non-metal oxides are acidic and will dissolve in water to form acids.

## Group Trends

![Illustration of groups and periods](/note-images/chemistry/groups-and-periods.png "Placeholder illustration: group trends")

Elements in the same group have similar properties. There are also trends within a group as you go down.

**Group 1 (alkali metals)** are soft, shiny metals that react vigorously with water to produce hydrogen gas and an alkaline solution. Reactivity increases as you go down the group. They have one electron in their outer shell.

**Group 7 (halogens)** are reactive non-metals. They are coloured and exist as molecules. Reactivity decreases as you go down the group. They have seven electrons in their outer shell. Fluorine is the most reactive halogen.

**Group 0 (noble gases)** are very unreactive. They have a full outer shell of electrons, which makes them stable. They exist as single atoms. Helium, neon, and argon are all noble gases. They have no tendency to gain, lose, or share electrons.
