## Exothermic Reactions

![Illustration of an exothermic reaction](/note-images/chemistry/exothermic-reactions.png "Placeholder illustration: exothermic reactions")

An **exothermic reaction** is a reaction that releases energy to its surroundings. This energy is often released as heat, causing the temperature of the surroundings to increase. Combustion is a good example of an exothermic reaction. Burning a fuel releases a large amount of heat energy. Oxidation reactions, such as rusting, are also exothermic, although they release energy much more slowly. Neutralisation is another exothermic reaction.

Examples of exothermic reactions include burning fuels, respiration in living organisms, and the reaction between acids and metals. Hand warmers and self-heating cans use exothermic reactions to generate heat.

## Endothermic Reactions

![Illustration of an endothermic reaction](/note-images/chemistry/endothermic-reactions.png "Placeholder illustration: endothermic reactions")

An **endothermic reaction** is a reaction that takes in energy from its surroundings. The energy is absorbed as heat, causing the temperature of the surroundings to decrease. The reaction between ammonium nitrate and water is endothermic. When these substances are mixed, the temperature drops noticeably. This effect is used in instant cold packs for treating injuries.

Thermal decomposition is an endothermic process because it requires energy to break the chemical bonds in the compound. Photosynthesis is also an endothermic reaction because plants absorb energy from sunlight to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.
