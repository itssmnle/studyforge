## Atoms

![Illustration of an atom](/note-images/chemistry/atomic-structure.png "Placeholder illustration: atoms")

**Atoms** are the tiny building blocks of all matter. They are incredibly small and cannot be seen with the naked eye. Every substance is made of atoms. The simple atomic model describes atoms as having a central nucleus surrounded by electrons. The nucleus contains protons and neutrons.

---

## Elements

![Illustration of elements and compounds](/note-images/chemistry/elements-and-compounds.png "Placeholder illustration: elements")

An **element** is a substance made up of only one type of atom. There are over 100 known elements. Each element has its own unique chemical symbol. For example, O represents oxygen, Fe represents iron, and Na represents sodium. Elements cannot be broken down into simpler substances by chemical reactions.

---

## Compounds

![Illustration of compounds](/note-images/chemistry/elements-and-compounds.png "Placeholder illustration: compounds")

A **compound** is a substance made of two or more different types of atoms chemically bonded together. The atoms are always combined in fixed proportions. Compounds have different properties from the elements that make them. For example, water (H₂O) is a compound made of hydrogen and oxygen atoms. Chemical formulae are used to represent compounds. The formula shows which elements are present and how many atoms of each are in the compound.

---

## Mixtures

![Illustration of a mixture](/note-images/chemistry/mixtures.png "Placeholder illustration: mixtures")

A **mixture** contains two or more elements or compounds that are mingled together but not chemically bonded. Unlike compounds, the substances in a mixture can be separated by physical methods. The properties of a mixture are the same as the properties of its components. Air, salt water, and soil are all examples of mixtures.

---

## Conservation of Mass

![Illustration of mass being conserved in a reaction](/note-images/chemistry/conservation-of-mass.png "Placeholder illustration: conservation of mass")

The law of **conservation of mass** states that in any physical change or chemical reaction, atoms are neither created nor destroyed. This means the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products. No atoms are lost or gained; they are simply rearranged.
