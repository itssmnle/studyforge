## The Reactivity Series

![Illustration of the reactivity series](/note-images/chemistry/reactivity-series.png "Placeholder illustration: the reactivity series")

The **reactivity series** is a list of metals arranged in order of their reactivity. The most reactive metals are at the top, and the least reactive are at the bottom. A metal's position in the series determines how it reacts with other substances and how it can be extracted.

The reactivity series helps predict displacement reactions. A more reactive metal will displace a less reactive metal from a compound.

The reactivity series includes, from most reactive to least reactive: Potassium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminium, Carbon, Zinc, Iron, Tin, Lead, Hydrogen, Copper, Silver, Gold.

Carbon is included in the reactivity series even though it is not a metal. This is because carbon is important for extracting metals.

## Metal Extraction

![Illustration of extracting metal](/note-images/chemistry/metal-extraction.png "Placeholder illustration: metal extraction")

Metals are often found in the Earth's crust combined with other elements in ores. The process of obtaining a metal from its ore is called **extraction**.

Metals that are less reactive than carbon can be extracted by heating their metal oxide with carbon. The carbon removes the oxygen from the metal oxide in a displacement reaction. This is called reduction. For example, iron oxide is heated with carbon to produce iron and carbon dioxide.

$$\ce{2Fe2O3 + 3C -> 4Fe + 3CO2}$$

Metals that are more reactive than carbon, such as aluminium, cannot be extracted using carbon. They must be extracted using electrolysis, which uses electricity to separate the metal from its compounds. This is more expensive than using carbon. Very unreactive metals, like gold and silver, are often found in their pure form and do not need to be extracted.

## Modern Materials

![Illustration of modern materials](/note-images/chemistry/modern-materials.png "Placeholder illustration: modern materials")

**Ceramics** are hard, strong, and heat-resistant materials. They are made from clay and other raw materials. Examples include bricks, tiles, and pottery. Ceramics do not conduct electricity.

**Polymers** are large molecules made from many smaller molecules linked together. Most polymers are plastics. They are lightweight, flexible, and can be moulded into different shapes. Examples include polythene, nylon, and PVC. Some polymers are biodegradable, meaning they can be broken down by microorganisms.

**Composites** are materials made by combining two or more different materials. The resulting material has properties that are better than the individual components. Fibreglass is a composite made of glass fibres embedded in a plastic resin. It is strong, lightweight, and used in boat building. Carbon fibre is another strong, lightweight composite used in sports equipment and aerospace.
