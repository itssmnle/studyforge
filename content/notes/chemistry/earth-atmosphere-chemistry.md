## Earth's Structure

![Illustration of Earth's layers](/note-images/chemistry/earth-structure.png "Placeholder illustration: Earth's structure")

The Earth is made up of several distinct layers.

The **inner core** is at the very centre. It is solid and made mostly of iron and nickel. Temperatures here are extremely high.

The **outer core** surrounds the inner core. It is liquid and also made of iron and nickel.

The **mantle** is the thickest layer. It is mostly solid, but can flow very slowly over long periods. This movement drives tectonic plate activity.

The **crust** is the thin, rocky outer layer. It is made of solid rock and is broken into large pieces called tectonic plates.

## The Rock Cycle

![Illustration of Earth materials](/note-images/chemistry/earth-structure.png "Placeholder illustration: the rock cycle")

The **rock cycle** shows how rocks are transformed from one type to another over time.

**Sedimentary rocks** are formed from sediments that are compressed and cemented together over millions of years. They often contain fossils. Examples include limestone, sandstone, and chalk.

**Igneous rocks** are formed when magma or lava cools and solidifies. If magma cools slowly underground, it forms rocks with large crystals, like granite. If lava cools quickly on the surface, it forms rocks with small crystals, like basalt.

**Metamorphic rocks** are formed when existing rocks are changed by great heat and pressure deep within the Earth. This process does not melt the rock, but changes its texture and mineral composition. Examples include marble, which forms from limestone, and slate, which forms from clay.

The rock cycle is driven by processes such as weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, heating, and pressure.

## The Carbon Cycle and Climate

![Illustration of climate change](/note-images/chemistry/climate-change.png "Placeholder illustration: the carbon cycle and climate")

The **carbon cycle** is the process by which carbon atoms are recycled through the environment. Carbon moves between the atmosphere, living organisms, the oceans, and the Earth's crust.

Carbon is taken out of the atmosphere by **photosynthesis** in plants. Plants use carbon dioxide to make glucose and oxygen. Carbon is returned to the atmosphere by **respiration** in plants and animals, which releases carbon dioxide. **Combustion** of fossil fuels also releases carbon dioxide. **Decomposition** of dead organisms releases carbon back into the soil and atmosphere. Over millions of years, dead organisms can become fossil fuels, storing carbon underground.

Human activities are affecting the carbon cycle. Burning fossil fuels releases large amounts of carbon dioxide into the atmosphere. Deforestation reduces the number of trees that can absorb carbon dioxide. This excess carbon dioxide is a **greenhouse gas**. It traps heat in the atmosphere, contributing to the greenhouse effect. This is leading to **climate change**, which includes global warming, rising sea levels, and more extreme weather events.
