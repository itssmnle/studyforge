## Pure Substances and Impurities

![Illustration of a pure substance](/note-images/chemistry/pure-substances.png "Placeholder illustration: pure substances and impurities")

A **pure substance** contains only one type of element or compound. It has a fixed melting point and boiling point. An **impure substance** contains more than one substance mixed together. Impurities lower the melting point and raise the boiling point. They also cause the substance to melt or boil over a range of temperatures rather than at a single specific point.

## Solubility

![Illustration of a substance dissolving in a mixture](/note-images/chemistry/mixtures.png "Placeholder illustration: solubility")

**Solubility** is a measure of how much of a substance, called the **solute**, can dissolve in a solvent to form a solution. The **solvent** is the liquid that does the dissolving. For most solids, solubility increases as temperature increases. This means more solute can dissolve in hot water than in cold water.

## Separation Techniques

![Illustration of separating a mixture](/note-images/chemistry/separating-mixtures.png "Placeholder illustration: separation techniques")

Mixtures can be separated using physical methods, which do not involve chemical reactions.

**Filtration** is used to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid. The mixture is poured through filter paper. The solid is trapped in the filter paper as the residue. The liquid passes through as the filtrate.

**Evaporation** is used to recover a soluble solid from a solution. The liquid is heated until it evaporates, leaving the solid behind.

**Crystallisation** is a more gentle method than evaporation. The solution is heated to evaporate some of the liquid, then left to cool. As it cools, crystals of the solid form.

**Simple distillation** is used to separate a solvent from a solution. The solution is heated. The solvent boils and evaporates, then is cooled and condensed back into a liquid in a separate container. This is how pure water can be obtained from salt water.

**Fractional distillation** is used to separate liquids with different boiling points. The mixture is heated. The liquid with the lowest boiling point evaporates first and is collected. The temperature is then increased to collect the next liquid. This is how crude oil is separated into different fractions.

**Chromatography** is used to separate mixtures of soluble substances, such as inks or dyes. A spot of the mixture is placed on chromatography paper. The paper is placed in a solvent. The solvent moves up the paper, carrying the different substances at different speeds. The substances separate into spots, forming a chromatogram.
