## 1. Diffusion, Osmosis, and Active Transport

### What does this mean?
Substances move into and out of cells across the cell membrane via three main processes:

* **Diffusion:** Net movement of particles from a region of **higher concentration** to **lower concentration** down a concentration gradient (passive).
* **Osmosis:** Net movement of **water molecules** from a region of **higher water potential** (dilute solution) to **lower water potential** (concentrated solution) across a **partially permeable membrane**.
* **Active Transport:** Movement of particles from **lower concentration** to **higher concentration** against a concentration gradient using **energy** from respiration.

Diffusion becomes faster when the concentration gradient is steeper, temperature is higher, surface area is larger, or diffusion distance is shorter. It supplies cells with oxygen and removes carbon dioxide. Osmosis allows roots to absorb water and controls water movement between cells.

### Method: Plant Cell Conditions in Osmosis
* **Turgid:** Plant cell in pure water / dilute solution; absorbs water and swells (high turgor pressure).
* **Flaccid:** Plant cell in equal concentration solution; no net movement of water.
* **Plasmolysed:** Plant cell in concentrated salt/sugar solution; loses water, cell membrane pulls away from cell wall.

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:**
Explain why root hair cells require active transport to absorb mineral ions from soil water.

**Answer:**
* Mineral ion concentration is higher inside the root cell than in the surrounding soil water.
* Ions must move **against the concentration gradient**.
* Diffusion cannot occur, so the cell must use **energy from respiration** via active transport.

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> When defining osmosis, always mention **water potential** and a **partially permeable membrane**. Never say "water diffuses from high water concentration" in GCSE exams!
