## 1. Enzyme Action and Factors Affecting Activity

### What does this mean?
An **enzyme** is a biological catalyst that speeds up metabolic reactions without being used up.
* Enzymes have a uniquely shaped **active site** that complements a specific **substrate** (Lock and Key Hypothesis).

![Lock and Key model showing enzyme active site binding with complementary substrate, forming enzyme-substrate complex](/science-images/Y9/enzyme%20action.webp "Enzyme action")

---

### Method: Temperature and pH Effects
1. **Low Temperature:** Particles have low kinetic energy; fewer successful collisions per second.
2. **Optimum Temperature / pH:** Maximum rate of reaction.
3. **High Temperature / Extreme pH:** Bonds holding enzyme shape break, active site changes shape $\rightarrow$ **Denaturation**. Substrate no longer fits.

---

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:**
Explain why human amylase stops working when heated above $60^\circ\text{C}$.

**Answer:**
* High thermal energy breaks bonds maintaining the 3D shape of the enzyme.
* The shape of the **active site changes**.
* The enzyme is **denatured**, so the substrate can no longer fit.

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Never say an enzyme "dies" or is "killed"! Enzymes are not living organisms; they are proteins. State that the enzyme becomes **denatured** and its active site changes shape.
