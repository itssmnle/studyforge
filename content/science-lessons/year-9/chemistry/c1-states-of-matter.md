## 1. Particle Theory and Changes of State

### What does this mean?
Matter exists in three states: **Solid**, **Liquid**, and **Gas**. Particle arrangement and energy determine physical properties:

* **Solid:** Particles tightly packed in a fixed regular lattice, vibrate in fixed positions.
* **Liquid:** Particles close together, random arrangement, slide past each other.
* **Gas:** Particles far apart, random arrangement, move rapidly in all directions.

![Particle arrangement diagrams for Solid, Liquid, and Gas alongside a Heating Curve graph showing melting and boiling plateaus](/science-images/Y9/particle%20theory.webp "Particle theory and changes of state")

---

### Method: State Changes & Kinetic Theory
* **Melting / Boiling:** Energy supplied breaks intermolecular forces; temperature remains constant during state changes.
* **Diffusion:** Movement of gas or liquid particles from high to low concentration.
* Lighter particles (lower relative molecular mass, $M_r$) diffuses **faster** than heavier particles.

---

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:**
Ammonia gas ($\text{NH}_3, M_r = 17$) and Hydrogen Chloride gas ($\text{HCl}, M_r = 36.5$) are released at opposite ends of a glass tube. Where will the white ring of ammonium chloride form?

**Answer:**
* $\text{NH}_3$ has a lower $M_r$ ($17$) than $\text{HCl}$ ($36.5$).
* Ammonia diffuses faster down the tube.
* The white ring forms **closer to the Hydrogen Chloride end**.

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> On heating/cooling curves, flat horizontal plateaus represent state changes! During these times, temperature stays constant because energy goes into overcoming attractive forces between particles rather than raising temperature.

## 2. Gas Temperature and Pressure

### What does this mean?
Gas pressure is caused by particles colliding with container walls. Heating gives particles more kinetic energy. In a sealed rigid container, faster and more frequent collisions increase pressure. If pressure is constant, heating a gas makes its volume increase. Increasing pressure at constant temperature decreases its volume.

![Diagram: Gas particles in three containers comparing low temperature, high temperature, and compressed high-pressure conditions]

### Method
1. State what changes about particle speed or spacing.
2. Describe the change in collision frequency or force.
3. Link the collisions to pressure or volume.

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:** Explain why the pressure in a sealed aerosol can rises when it is heated.

**Answer:** The particles gain kinetic energy and move faster. They collide with the walls more frequently and with greater force, so the pressure increases.

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Explain gas behaviour using particles and collisions, not by saying that particles themselves expand.
