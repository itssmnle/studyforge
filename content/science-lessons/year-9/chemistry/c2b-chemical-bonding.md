## 1. Ionic, Covalent, and Metallic Bonding

### What does this mean?
Atoms form bonds to achieve a stable full outer shell of electrons:

1. **Ionic Bonding:** Transfer of electrons from **metal to non-metal** forming positive and negative ions held by strong electrostatic forces in a giant lattice.
2. **Covalent Bonding:** Sharing pairs of electrons between **non-metal atoms**.
3. **Metallic Bonding:** Lattice of positive metal ions surrounded by a **sea of delocalised electrons**.

### Method: Explaining Properties by Structure
* **Ionic Compounds:** High melting points (strong ionic bonds), conduct electricity when **molten or dissolved** (ions free to move).
* **Simple Covalent:** Low boiling points (weak intermolecular forces between molecules).
* **Diamond:** Giant covalent structure; each Carbon bonded to $4$ others $\rightarrow$ extremely hard, non-conductor.
* **Graphite:** Each Carbon bonded to $3$ others in layers with delocalised electrons $\rightarrow$ soft/slippery, **conducts electricity**.
* **Metals:** Delocalised electrons carry charge and thermal energy; layers of ions can slide, making metals malleable and ductile while metallic attraction remains.

---

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:**
Explain why graphite can conduct electricity but diamond cannot.

**Answer:**
* In graphite, each carbon atom forms $3$ covalent bonds, leaving $1$ **delocalised electron** per atom that is free to move along layers.
* In diamond, all $4$ outer electrons are involved in covalent bonds, so there are **no free electrons**.

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> When explaining why simple covalent molecules melt easily, state that **weak intermolecular forces** between molecules are broken, NOT the strong covalent bonds inside the molecules!
