## 1. Measuring Physical Quantities

### What does this mean?
Reliable measurements use suitable instruments and repeated readings. Length is measured with a ruler, volume with a measuring cylinder or by displacement, and time with a stopwatch. Very small values are more accurately found by measuring several identical quantities together and dividing.

![Diagram: Measuring a pendulum period over ten oscillations and measuring an irregular object's volume by water displacement]

### Method
1. Choose an instrument with a suitable range and resolution.
2. Read scales at eye level to avoid parallax error.
3. Repeat measurements and calculate a mean.
4. For a repeated quantity, use:
$$\text{single value}=\frac{\text{total measured value}}{\text{number of values}}$$

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:** Ten pendulum oscillations take $16.4\text{ s}$. Calculate the period.

**Answer:**
$$T=\frac{16.4}{10}=1.64\text{ s}$$

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Time several oscillations, then divide. Timing one oscillation gives a much larger percentage uncertainty.

## 2. Speed, Acceleration, and Motion Graphs

### What does this mean?
* **Scalar:** Quantity with magnitude only (e.g., speed, distance, mass).
* **Vector:** Quantity with magnitude AND direction (e.g., velocity, acceleration, force, weight).

$$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} \quad \Big| \quad \text{Acceleration } a = \frac{v - u}{t}$$

![Diagram: Speed-time graph showing constant acceleration, steady speed, and deceleration with highlighted shaded area representing distance]

---

### Method: Motion Graphs
* **Distance–Time Graph:** Gradient $= \text{Speed}$.
* **Speed–Time Graph:** Gradient $= \text{Acceleration}$.
* **Area under Speed–Time Graph:** Total Distance Travelled.
* **Free fall:** Near Earth's surface, objects accelerate at approximately $9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ when air resistance is negligible.

---

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:**
A car accelerates uniformly from rest ($0\text{ m/s}$) to $20\text{ m/s}$ in $5\text{ seconds}$. Calculate its acceleration and distance travelled.

**Answer:**
1. Acceleration:
   $$a = \frac{20 - 0}{5} = 4\text{ m/s}^2$$
2. Distance (Area of triangle under graph):
   $$\text{Distance} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 20 = 50\text{ m}$$

Answer: **$a = 4\text{ m/s}^2$, $\text{Distance} = 50\text{ m}$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> A gradient needs a triangle using values from the line. The area under a speed–time graph gives distance, not acceleration.

## 3. Mass, Weight, and Resultant Forces

### What does this mean?
* **Mass ($m$):** Amount of matter in an object ($\text{kg}$).
* **Weight ($W$):** Gravitational force acting on a mass ($\text{N}$).

$$W = m g \quad (g = 9.8\text{ N/kg or } 10\text{ N/kg on Earth})$$

* **Newton's Second Law:**

$$F = m a$$

![Diagram: Free body diagram showing balanced and unbalanced resultant forces acting on an accelerating object]

Forces can change an object's speed, direction, size, or shape. **Friction** and **drag** oppose motion. When the resultant force is zero, an object remains at rest or continues at constant velocity. When the resultant force is not zero, the object accelerates in its direction.

### Method
1. Choose a positive direction.
2. Add forces in that direction and subtract opposing forces.
3. If the resultant is zero, velocity is constant.
4. Otherwise use $a=F/m$.

---

### Worked Example & Answer

**Question:**
A car of mass $1000\text{ kg}$ has a forward driving force of $3000\text{ N}$ and a resistive friction force of $1000\text{ N}$. Calculate the acceleration.

**Answer:**
1. Resultant Force $F = 3000 - 1000 = 2000\text{ N}$
2. $F = ma \implies 2000 = 1000 \times a$
3. $a = \frac{2000}{1000} = 2\text{ m/s}^2$

Answer: **$2\text{ m/s}^2$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Mass never changes depending on location, but weight changes if gravitational field strength $g$ changes! E.g., your mass is the same on the Moon, but your weight is much less.
