## 1. The Human Skeleton and Joints

### What does this mean?
The human skeleton is an endoskeleton made of $206$ bones that provides a rigid framework for the body.

![Diagram: Labeled skeleton diagram highlighting Cranium, Ribcage, Vertebral Column, Pelvis, Femur, and Synovial Joint structure]

---

### Key Concepts & Functions

#### Four Main Functions of the Skeleton:
1. **Support:** Holds the body upright and maintains shape against gravity.
2. **Protection:** Shields vital internal organs (Cranium protects brain; Ribcage protects heart and lungs; Vertebrae protect spinal cord).
3. **Movement:** Acts as a system of levers pulled by skeletal muscles.
4. **Blood Cell Production:** Red and white blood cells are produced in the **bone marrow** inside hollow bones.

#### Types of Joints:
| Joint type | Movement and example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Fixed / Immovable Joints** | Bones fused together for protection, such as sutures in the cranium. |
| **Hinge Joints** | Allow back-and-forth movement in **one plane**, such as the elbow and knee. |
| **Ball and Socket Joints** | Allow rotational movement in **all directions** ($360^\circ$), such as the hip and shoulder. |

#### Structure of a Synovial Joint:
| Structure | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cartilage** | Smooth, slippery tissue covering bone ends; reduces friction and absorbs shock. |
| **Synovial Fluid** | Lubricating liquid inside the joint capsule. |
| **Ligaments** | Tough, elastic connective tissue connecting **bone to bone** to stabilise the joint. |
| **Tendons** | Inflexible connective tissue connecting **muscle to bone** to transmit pulling force. |

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
State the connective tissue that attaches muscle to bone, and explain its physical property requirement.

**Answer:**
Connective tissue: **Tendon**.  
Requirement: It must be **inelastic / non-stretchy** so that when the muscle contracts, the pulling force is transferred directly to move the bone without the tendon stretching.

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Remember the distinction: **Ligaments** connect **L**ike-to-like (**b**one to **b**one). **Tendons** connect **M**uscle to **b**one!

## 2. Biomechanics, Levers, and Antagonistic Muscles

### What does this mean?
**Biomechanics** studies how forces act on and within the biological structures of the body. Muscles can only **contract and pull**; they cannot push. Therefore, muscles work in **antagonistic pairs** around a pivot (joint).

![Diagram: Diagram of the human arm showing Biceps contracting (flexion) and Triceps relaxing to lift forearm around elbow pivot]

---

### Key Concepts & Calculations

#### Antagonistic Muscle Action (Arm Movement):
* **To BEND the arm (Flexion):** The **Biceps contracts** (shortens and thickens) while the **Triceps relaxes**.
* **To STRAIGHTEN the arm (Extension):** The **Triceps contracts** while the **Biceps relaxes**.

#### Biomechanics of Moments:
A joint acts as a **pivot (fulcrum)**. The turning effect created by a muscle force is called a **moment**:

$$\text{Moment (Nm)} = \text{Force (N)} \times \text{Perpendicular Distance from Pivot (m)}$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
A bicep muscle exerts an upward force of $150\text{ N}$ at a distance of $0.04\text{ m}$ from the elbow joint pivot. Calculate the moment generated by the bicep muscle.

**Answer:**
$$\text{Moment} = \text{Force} \times \text{Distance} = 150\text{ N} \times 0.04\text{ m} = 6\text{ Nm}$$

Answer: **$6\text{ Nm}$**
