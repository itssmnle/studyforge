## 1. Microscopes, Magnification, and Cell Structures

### What does this mean?
Cells are the microscopic structural and functional units of all living organisms. Because most cells are too small to be seen with the naked eye, scientists use **light microscopes** to magnify them.

![Diagram: Labeled diagram of a light microscope showing eyepiece lens, objective lenses, stage, light source, coarse focus, and fine focus knobs]

---

### Key Concepts & Methods

#### 1. Parts of a Light Microscope:
* **Eyepiece Lens:** The lens you look through (usually $\times 10$ magnification).
* **Objective Lenses:** Swappable lenses of varying power ($\times 4, \times 10, \times 40$).
* **Stage & Stage Clips:** Holds the glass slide in place.
* **Coarse & Fine Focus Knobs:** Adjust focus to bring the image into sharp detail.

#### 2. Preparing a Specimen (e.g. Onion Cells):
1. Peel a thin, single-cell layer of epidermal tissue using forceps.
2. Place flat on a clean glass slide and add a drop of stain (**Iodine solution** for plant cells, **Methylene blue** for animal cells).
3. Lower a **coverslip** slowly at an angle using a mounted needle to avoid trapping **air bubbles**.

#### 3. Calculating Magnification:

$$\text{Total Magnification} = \text{Eyepiece Magnification} \times \text{Objective Magnification}$$

$$\text{Image Size } (I) = \text{Actual Size } (A) \times \text{Magnification } (M)$$

$$\text{Unit Conversion: } 1\text{ millimetre (mm)} = 1000\text{ micrometres }(\mu\text{m})$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
An image of an onion cell under a microscope measures $45\text{ mm}$ in length. The microscope has an eyepiece lens of $\times 10$ and an objective lens of $\times 40$. Calculate the actual length of the cell in micrometres ($\mu\text{m}$).

**Answer:**
1. Find total magnification: $10 \times 40 = 400$
2. Convert image size to $\mu\text{m}$: $45\text{ mm} \times 1000 = 45,000\text{ }\mu\text{m}$
3. Calculate actual size: $A = \frac{I}{M} = \frac{45,000}{400} = 112.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$

Answer: **$112.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> Always convert your image size measurement from $\text{mm}$ to $\mu\text{m}$ (by multiplying by $1000$) **before** dividing by magnification!

## 2. Animal, Plant, and Unicellular Organisms

### What does this mean?
* **Animal Cells:** Contain nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, and ribosomes.
* **Plant Cells:** Contain all animal cell organelles **plus** a cellulose **cell wall**, **permanent vacuole**, and **chloroplasts**.
* **Unicellular Organisms:** Organisms consisting of a single cell that performs all life functions independently (e.g., *Amoeba*, *Euglena*).

![Diagram: Labeled cell comparison showing Animal Cell, Plant Cell, Amoeba (with pseudopodia), and Euglena (with flagellum and chloroplasts)]

---

### Key Organelle Functions
| Organelle | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Nucleus** | Controls cell activities and stores genetic material (DNA). |
| **Cytoplasm** | Jelly-like fluid where chemical metabolic reactions occur. |
| **Cell Membrane** | Selectively permeable layer controlling entry and exit of substances. |
| **Mitochondria** | Site of **aerobic respiration** to release energy for the cell. |
| **Ribosomes** | Site of **protein synthesis**. |
| **Cell Wall** | Rigid outer boundary made of **cellulose**; prevents bursting and provides support. |
| **Permanent Vacuole** | Filled with cell sap; maintains turgor pressure. |
| **Chloroplasts** | Contain green **chlorophyll** pigment to absorb light for **photosynthesis**. |

#### Unicellular Organisms:
| Organism | Features and adaptations |
| :--- | :--- |
| *Amoeba* | Freshwater protozoan. Uses finger-like projections called **pseudopodia** ("false feet") for movement and engulfing food (**phagocytosis**). Possesses a **contractile vacuole** to pump out excess water. |
| *Euglena* | Autotrophic and heterotrophic unicellular organism. Possesses a whip-like **flagellum** for swimming, an **eyespot** to **detect light direction**, and **chloroplasts** for photosynthesis. |

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
State two cell structures present in a *Euglena* that are also present in a plant cell, but absent in an animal cell.

**Answer:**
1. **Chloroplasts** (used for photosynthesis).
2. **Cell Wall** (though *Euglena* has a flexible pellicle, chloroplasts and vacuole structures represent plant-like characteristics).

## 3. Diffusion and Specialised Cells

### What does this mean?
* **Diffusion:** The net movement of particles from a region of **higher concentration** to a region of **lower concentration** down a concentration gradient (a passive process requiring no cellular energy).
* **Specialised Cells:** Cells that have developed specific structural adaptations to perform a specialized function efficiently.

![Diagram: Specialized cells showing 1) Red Blood Cell (biconcave), 2) Sperm Cell (flagellum, acrosome), 3) Root Hair Cell (extended projection), 4) Ciliated Epithelial Cell (cilia)]

---

### Key Concepts & Specialised Adaptations

#### Factors Increasing Diffusion Rate:
1. **Steeper Concentration Gradient:** Larger difference between high and low concentration.
2. **Higher Temperature:** Increases kinetic energy of particles, making them move faster.
3. **Larger Surface Area:** Provides more space for particles to pass through.

#### Specialised Cell Structure & Function:
| Specialised cell | Function | Adaptations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Red Blood Cell** | Transports oxygen. | **Biconcave disc shape** for a large surface area to volume ratio; contains **hemoglobin**; **no nucleus** to maximise space for oxygen. |
| **Sperm Cell** | Fertilises the female egg. | **Flagellum (tail)** for swimming; packed with **mitochondria** for energy; contains an **acrosome** with digestive enzymes. |
| **Root Hair Cell** | Absorbs water and mineral ions from soil. | **Long hair-like extension** provides a massive surface area; thin cell wall; no chloroplasts because it is underground. |
| **Ciliated Epithelial Cell** | Sweeps mucus and trapped dust out of airways. | Covered in microscopic hair-like **cilia** that beat in a rhythmic motion. |

## 4. Principles and Levels of Organisation

### What does this mean?
Multicellular organisms are organized into a hierarchical structure of increasing complexity.

![Diagram: Pyramid diagram of levels of organization: Organelle -> Cell -> Tissue -> Organ -> Organ System -> Organism]

---

### Hierarchy Steps:
| Level | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Organelle** | Specialised structure within a cell. | Nucleus, mitochondrion |
| **Cell** | Basic building block of life. | Muscle cell, epithelial cell |
| **Tissue** | Group of similar cells working together. | Muscle tissue, xylem tissue |
| **Organ** | Different tissues working together. | Heart, stomach, leaf |
| **Organ System** | Organs working together. | Circulatory system, digestive system |
| **Organism** | Individual living creature made of interconnected organ systems. | Human, oak tree |

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Classify the following items into their correct level of organisation: *Heart*, *Red Blood Cell*, *Circulatory System*, *Mitochondrion*.

**Answer:**
* **Mitochondrion:** Organelle
* **Red Blood Cell:** Cell
* **Heart:** Organ
* **Circulatory System:** Organ System
