## 1. Pure Substances, Solutions, and Diffusion

### What does this mean?
* **Pure Substance:** Consists of only one type of element or compound. Has a **sharp, fixed melting point** and boiling point.
* **Impure Substance (Mixture):** Contains multiple substances. Melts and boils **over a range of temperatures**.
* **Solution Terms:**
  * **Solute:** The solid that dissolves (e.g. Salt).
  * **Solvent:** The liquid that dissolves the solute (e.g. Water).
  * **Solution:** The homogeneous mixture formed ($\text{Solute} + \text{Solvent}$).
  * **Saturated Solution:** A solution where no more solute can dissolve at a specific temperature.

## 2. Separation Techniques

### What does this mean?
Mixtures can be separated into pure components using physical separation methods based on differences in physical properties.

![Diagram: Four-panel separation techniques diagram showing Filtration, Evaporation, Distillation, and Chromatography]

---

### Summary of Separation Techniques

| Method | Used to Separate | Key Mechanism / Property |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Filtration** | Insoluble solid from a liquid | Filter paper traps larger residue; liquid filtrate passes through pores |
| **Evaporation** | Soluble solute from a liquid solution | Solvent is boiled off; dissolved solute crystallizes |
| **Simple Distillation** | Pure liquid solvent from a solution | Liquid evaporates, travels down a **Liebig condenser**, and condenses |
| **Paper Chromatography** | Mixture of soluble dyes / inks | Components separate based on relative solubility in solvent |

$$\text{Retention Factor } (R_f) = \frac{\text{Distance moved by dye spot}}{\text{Distance moved by solvent front}}$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
In a paper chromatography run, a blue dye spot travels $3.2\text{ cm}$ from the pencil origin line, while the solvent front travels $8.0\text{ cm}$. Calculate the $R_f$ value.

**Answer:**
$$R_f = \frac{3.2\text{ cm}}{8.0\text{ cm}} = 0.40$$

Answer: **$0.40$**

> [!INFO] **Examiner Tips and Tricks**
> $R_f$ values are always numbers between $0$ and $1$ and have **no units**. Always draw the starting baseline in **pencil**, as ink would dissolve in the solvent and contaminate the chromatogram!
