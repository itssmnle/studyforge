## 1. The Particle Model and States of Matter

### What does this mean?
The **particle model** explains the physical properties of solids, liquids, and gases based on the arrangement, movement, and forces of attraction between particles.

![Diagram: Three box diagrams comparing particle arrangement and movement in Solid (regular lattice), Liquid (random, touching), and Gas (widely spaced)]

---

### Comparison Table of States

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arrangement** | Regular repeating lattice | Random arrangement | Random arrangement |
| **Spacing** | Tightly packed touching | Close together touching | Far apart |
| **Movement** | Vibrate about fixed points | Slide past each other | Move fast in all directions |
| **Intermolecular Forces** | Very strong | Moderate | Extremely weak |
| **Shape & Volume** | Fixed shape & volume | Fixed volume, fills container base | Variable shape & volume |
| **Compressibility** | Incompressible | Incompressible | Highly compressible |

## 2. Changes of State and Heating Curves

### What does this mean?
When a substance gains or loses thermal energy, its state changes. State changes are **physical changes** (reversible, no new chemical substances formed).

![Diagram: Heating curve graph plotting Temperature vs Time showing horizontal plateaus during melting and boiling]

---

### Key Processes & Kinetic Theory:
* **Melting:** Solid $\rightarrow$ Liquid (energy breaks rigid lattice).
* **Freezing:** Liquid $\rightarrow$ Solid.
* **Evaporation / Boiling:** Liquid $\rightarrow$ Gas.
* **Condensation:** Gas $\rightarrow$ Liquid.
* **Sublimation:** Solid $\rightarrow$ Gas directly (e.g. Dry Ice $\text{CO}_2$).
* **Deposition:** Gas $\rightarrow$ Solid directly.

> [!INFO] **Heating Curve Plateaus**
> During a state change (e.g. melting or boiling), the temperature graph flattens into a **horizontal plateau**. Thermal energy supplied is being used to overcome **intermolecular forces** rather than increasing particle kinetic energy/temperature!

## 3. Gas Pressure

### What does this mean?
**Gas pressure** is caused by gas particles rapidly colliding with the internal walls of their container, exerting a net force per unit area.

![Diagram: Gas container showing gas particle collision vectors against container walls]

---

### Factors Affecting Gas Pressure:
1. **Temperature:** Heating a gas increases particle average kinetic energy. Particles move faster, colliding with container walls **more frequently and with greater force** $\rightarrow$ **Pressure increases**.
2. **Volume:** Decreasing container volume packs gas particles into a smaller space. Collisions with walls occur **more frequently** $\rightarrow$ **Pressure increases**.
3. **Number of Particles:** Adding more gas particles increases total collision frequency $\rightarrow$ **Pressure increases**.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Explain using particle theory why a bicycle tyre feels harder on a hot sunny day.

**Answer:**
* Thermal energy transfers to air particles inside the tyre, increasing their kinetic energy and speed.
* Gas particles collide with the rigid inner tyre walls **more frequently and with greater force**.
* This increases the internal gas pressure, making the tyre feel firmer.
