## 1. Energy Stores, Transfers, and Conservation

### What does this mean?
The **Law of Conservation of Energy** states that energy cannot be created or destroyed; it can only be transferred from one store to another.

![Diagram: Energy transfer diagram of a bouncing ball showing energy shifting between Gravitational Potential, Kinetic, Elastic, and Thermal stores]

---

### Key Concepts

#### 8 Energy Stores:
1. **Kinetic ($E_k$):** Stored in moving objects.
2. **Gravitational Potential ($E_p$):** Stored in objects raised above ground.
3. **Chemical:** Stored in food, fuels, and batteries.
4. **Thermal:** Stored in hot objects (particle kinetic energy).
5. **Elastic Potential:** Stored in stretched or compressed materials.
6. **Electrostatic:** Stored between charged particles.
7. **Magnetic:** Stored between magnetic poles.
8. **Nuclear:** Stored inside atomic nuclei.

#### 4 Energy Transfer Pathways:
* **Mechanical Work:** Force moving through a distance.
* **Electrical Work:** Charge moving through a potential difference.
* **Heating:** Temperature gradient transfer.
* **Radiation:** Light or sound waves.

## 2. Power, Efficiency, and Energy Resources

### What does this mean?
* **Power:** The rate at which energy is transferred or work is done ($1\text{ Watt} = 1\text{ Joule per second}$).

$$\text{Power (W)} = \frac{\text{Energy Transferred (J)}}{\text{Time Taken (s)}} \quad \Big| \quad P = \frac{E}{t}$$

$$\text{Efficiency (\%)} = \frac{\text{Useful Energy Output}}{\text{Total Energy Input}} \times 100$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
A $100\text{ W}$ light bulb converts $1200\text{ J}$ of electrical energy into $180\text{ J}$ of useful light energy and $1020\text{ J}$ of wasted thermal energy. Calculate:
1. How long the bulb was switched on.
2. The percentage efficiency of the bulb.

**Answer:**
1. Time: $t = \frac{E}{P} = \frac{1200\text{ J}}{100\text{ W}} = 12\text{ s}$
2. Efficiency: $\text{Efficiency} = \frac{180}{1200} \times 100 = 15\%$

Answer: **1) $12\text{ s}$, 2) $15\%$**
