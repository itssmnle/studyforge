## 1. Speed Calculations and Distance–Time Graphs

### What does this mean?
**Speed** is a scalar quantity measuring the distance covered per unit time.

$$\text{Speed } (v) = \frac{\text{Distance } (d)}{\text{Time } (t)} \quad \Big| \quad d = v \times t \quad \Big| \quad t = \frac{d}{v}$$

![Diagram: Distance-time graph showing 1) Horizontal line (stationary), 2) Straight upward line (constant speed), 3) Curved line (acceleration)]

---

### Distance–Time Graph Rules:
* **Gradient (Slope):** Represents **Speed** ($\text{Gradient} = \frac{\text{Change in } y}{\text{Change in } x} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$).
* **Horizontal Flat Line:** Object is **stationary** (speed $= 0\text{ m/s}$).
* **Straight Sloping Line:** Constant, steady speed.
* **Steeper Gradient:** Faster speed.
* **Curved Upward Line:** Object is **accelerating**.

---

### Relative Motion:
* Objects moving in **opposite directions** towards each other:

$$\text{Relative Speed} = v_1 + v_2$$

* Objects moving in the **same direction**:

$$\text{Relative Speed} = v_1 - v_2$$

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Car A travels at $25\text{ m/s}$ and Car B travels at $15\text{ m/s}$ in the same direction along a straight road. Calculate their relative speed.

**Answer:**
$$\text{Relative Speed} = 25\text{ m/s} - 15\text{ m/s} = 10\text{ m/s}$$

Answer: **$10\text{ m/s}$**
