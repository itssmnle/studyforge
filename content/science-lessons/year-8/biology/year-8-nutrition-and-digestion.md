## 1. Healthy Diet, Nutrient Deficiency, and Digestion

### What does this mean?
A **balanced diet** provides the correct proportions of the 7 essential nutrients needed for cellular energy, growth, and health.

![Diagram: Labeled human digestive tract showing mouth, esophagus, stomach, liver, pancreas, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, and anus]

---

### The 7 Essential Nutrients

1. **Carbohydrates:** Provide primary cellular energy (starch and simple sugars).
2. **Proteins:** Essential for growth and tissue repair.
3. **Lipids (Fats & Oils):** Provide long-term energy storage and thermal insulation.
4. **Vitamins:** Organic compounds required in small amounts (Vitamin C prevents **scurvy**; Vitamin D prevents **rickets**).
5. **Minerals:** Inorganic ions (Iron needed for hemoglobin to prevent **anemia**; Calcium for bones/teeth).
6. **Water:** Solvent for metabolic reactions and transport.
7. **Fibre:** Indigestible plant material providing bulk for **peristalsis** (muscular contractions of gut walls).

## 2. Digestive Organs, Enzymes, and Gut Bacteria

### What does this mean?
Digestion is the breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into small, soluble molecules that can be absorbed across the intestinal wall into the bloodstream.

---

### Key Organ Functions & Enzymes:
* **Mouth:** Mechanical breakdown by teeth; saliva contains **amylase** (breaks starch into maltose/glucose).
* **Stomach:** Muscular bag that churns food with **hydrochloric acid ($\text{pH } 2$)** to kill pathogens; contains **pepsin** (protease enzyme).
* **Liver & Gallbladder:** Produces and stores **bile**. Bile **emulsifies fats** (breaks large oil droplets into small droplets to increase surface area) and neutralises stomach acid.
* **Small Intestine (Ileum):** Digestion completed by pancreatic enzymes; absorbed via millions of microscopic **villi**.
* **Gut Bacteria:** Commensal bacteria in the large intestine that digest cellulose, produce essential **Vitamin K**, and prevent harmful bacterial infections.

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Explain how the structure of a villus is adapted for efficient absorption of nutrients in the small intestine.

**Answer:**
1. **Huge Surface Area:** Microvilli coverage increases total absorption area.
2. **Thin Barrier:** Wall is only **one cell thick**, providing a short diffusion pathway.
3. **Rich Blood Supply:** Dense capillary network maintains a steep concentration gradient.
