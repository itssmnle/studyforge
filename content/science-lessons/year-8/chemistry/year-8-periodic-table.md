## 1. Structure of the Periodic Table and Group Trends

### What does this mean?
The **Periodic Table** arranges elements in order of increasing atomic number.
* **Groups (Columns):** Elements have the same number of outer shell electrons and show **similar chemical properties**.
* **Periods (Rows):** Represent the number of occupied electron shells.

### Key Group Trends

#### Group 1 (Alkali Metals: $\text{Li}, \text{Na}, \text{K}$):
* Soft, low-density metals. React vigorously with water to form alkaline hydroxides and hydrogen gas:

$$2\text{Na} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{NaOH} + \text{H}_2$$

* Reactivity **INCREASES down Group 1**.

#### Group 7 (Halogens: $\text{F}_2, \text{Cl}_2, \text{Br}_2, \text{I}_2$):
* Diatomic non-metals. Reactivity **DECREASES down Group 7**.
* A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive halogen from its halide salt solution:

$$\text{Cl}_2 + 2\text{KBr} \rightarrow 2\text{KCl} + \text{Br}_2$$

#### Group 0 (Noble Gases: $\text{He}, \text{Ne}, \text{Ar}$):
* Extremely unreactive (inert) non-metal gases because they possess **full outer electron shells**.
