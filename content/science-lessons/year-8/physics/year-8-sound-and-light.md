## 1. Wave Properties, Sound, and Hearing

### What does this mean?
* **Transverse Waves:** Oscillations are **perpendicular ($90^\circ$)** to the direction of energy transfer (e.g. Light, Water ripples).
* **Longitudinal Waves:** Oscillations are **parallel** to the direction of energy transfer (e.g. Sound waves).

![Diagram: Wave diagram illustrating Amplitude (peak height), Wavelength (peak to peak), Frequency, and Oscilloscope pitch traces]

---

### Sound & Ear Mechanics:
* **Loudness:** Determined by **Amplitude** (larger height $=$ louder sound).
* **Pitch:** Determined by **Frequency** in Hertz ($\text{Hz}$) (more waves per second $=$ higher pitch).
* **Speed of Sound:** $\sim 330\text{ m/s}$ in air. Sound **requires particles** and cannot travel through a vacuum.
* **Hearing Pathway:** Sound waves enter ear canal $\rightarrow$ **Eardrum vibrates** $\rightarrow$ **Ossicles** amplify vibrations $\rightarrow$ **Cochlea** hair cells convert vibrations to electrical signals $\rightarrow$ **Auditory nerve** sends signals to brain. Human hearing range: $20\text{ Hz} - 20,000\text{ Hz}$.

## 2. Light, Reflection, and Refraction

### What does this mean?
Light is a transverse wave that travels in straight lines at $300,000,000\text{ m/s}$ ($3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$).

![Diagram: Ray diagrams showing 1) Law of Reflection (i = r) at a plane mirror, and 2) Refraction bending light towards normal in a glass block]

---

### Key Optics Rules:
* **Law of Reflection:** The **Angle of Incidence ($i$)** equals the **Angle of Reflection ($r$)**, measured relative to the normal line ($90^\circ$ perpendicular line to mirror surface).
* **Refraction:** When light enters a denser medium (e.g. air into glass), it **slows down and bends TOWARDS the normal line**. When exiting into a less dense medium, it speeds up and bends **AWAY from the normal**.
* **Dispersion:** White light passing through a glass prism refracts at different angles according to wavelength, splitting into the visible spectrum: **Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet (ROYGBIV)**.

---

### Worked Example

**Question:**
Explain why a red apple appears black when viewed under pure green light.

**Answer:**
* A red apple surface only reflects red light wavelengths and absorbs all other colors.
* Green light falling on the red apple is completely **absorbed**.
* No light is reflected to the observer's eye, so the apple appears **black**.
